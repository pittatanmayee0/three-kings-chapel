THREE KING CHAPEL

The first time I heard about the Three Kings Chapel in Goa, I thought it was just another old church surrounded by stories. But the more I learned about it, the more mysterious the place seemed. The chapel stands alone on a hill near Cuelim, surrounded by thick greenery and offering a beautiful view of the countryside. During the day, the place looks peaceful, but after sunset, it is said to have a completely different atmosphere. According to local legend, the hill was once ruled by three Portuguese kings who constantly fought among themselves for power. Their rivalry eventually became so dangerous that one of the kings allegedly poisoned the other two and then, overwhelmed by guilt and fear of revenge, took his own life. The story says that their spirits never truly left the hill. As I read more about the chapel, I came across several strange stories connected to the place. Some locals believe that visitors can sometimes feel an unexplained presence around the chapel, especially when they stay there after dark. There are also stories of people hearing unusual sounds or feeling as though someone is watching them even when nobody else is around. One of the most chilling beliefs is that the spirits of the three kings still wander around the hill, guarding the place where their story supposedly ended. Whether these stories are true or simply legends passed down through generations, they have given Three Kings Chapel a reputation as one of Goa's most mysterious locations. The isolated hill, the old chapel, and the silence surrounding it make the place feel strangely different from ordinary tourist spots. Standing there, I could not help wondering how many stories the quiet walls had witnessed over the years. Maybe the Three Kings Chapel is simply an abandoned piece of history—or perhaps, after nightfall, the hill still remembers its three forgotten kings.

As I continued looking into the story of Three Kings Chapel, my curiosity slowly turned into uneasiness. There was something strangely fascinating about a chapel standing alone on a quiet hill, surrounded by trees and silence. I imagined what it would feel like to climb the hill when the sun was beginning to disappear and the last rays of light were falling across the old walls. The peaceful surroundings almost seemed misleading because behind that calm atmosphere was a legend filled with betrayal, murder, guilt, and death. According to the stories passed down by locals, the three kings who once fought for control of the area left behind more than just a tragic history. People began associating the hill with mysterious occurrences, and the chapel gradually gained a reputation for being haunted. The most interesting part was that many stories seemed to focus on the feeling of being watched. Visitors have claimed that the atmosphere suddenly becomes uncomfortable when they remain there for too long, while others have described hearing strange sounds despite finding nobody nearby. I could not help imagining myself standing alone beside the chapel, listening carefully to every sound around me. The wind moving through the trees could easily sound like footsteps, while the creaking branches might resemble whispers in the darkness. Yet that was exactly what made the place so unsettling—the possibility that something ordinary could suddenly feel supernatural. As I learned more, I realized that the legend of the three kings had become deeply connected with the identity of the hill itself. Whether the strange stories are genuine paranormal experiences, exaggerated tales, or simply the result of an eerie environment, nobody can completely prove what happens there after dark. The chapel remains a mysterious reminder of an old legend, and perhaps that uncertainty is what makes Three Kings Chapel so fascinating. Some places are frightening because of what we know about them, but this place seemed frightening because of what we can never truly know.

HISTORY OF THREE KINGS CHAPEL

The deeper I went into the history of Three Kings Chapel, the more I realized that the real story was almost as mysterious as the legend itself. The chapel stands on Cuelim Hill in Cansaulim, South Goa, and its documented history goes back to 1599, when it was founded by Father Gonçalo Carvalho during the Portuguese period. It was originally associated with the name Our Lady of Remedies and was built as a place of Catholic worship. Even today, the chapel remains connected with the local community and the traditional Feast of the Three Kings celebrated every year on January 6. But somewhere along the way, another story became attached to this quiet hilltop. According to local folklore, three powerful kings once controlled the area and were constantly fighting among themselves. Each wanted complete authority, and their rivalry eventually became so intense that one of them, commonly named in the legend as King Holger Alvunger, supposedly decided to eliminate the other two. He invited them to a meal at the hill and secretly poisoned their food. The plan succeeded, but the victory did not last. When people discovered what had happened, anger spread through the area. Realizing that the consequences were coming for him, the king is said to have consumed the remaining poison himself. According to the legend, all three kings died because of the deadly rivalry and were eventually buried near the chapel. And this is where the haunting supposedly began. Locals began telling stories that the spirits of the three kings never left the hill. Instead, they were believed to wander around the chapel, as if still guarding the territory they had once fought so desperately to control. Over time, visitors began associating the lonely hill, strange nighttime sounds, and the isolated chapel with the three restless spirits. Stories of an unexplained presence around the chapel after dark helped strengthen its reputation as one of Goa's most mysterious locations. But there is a fascinating twist. Historical accounts do not support the idea that three Portuguese kings actually ruled from this hill. Portuguese Goa was administered through colonial authorities rather than three kings living at the chapel. This means the haunting story is best understood as folklore rather than established history. Still, that did not make the legend disappear. In fact, the uncertainty made it even more fascinating to me. A real sixteenth-century chapel, a lonely hilltop, centuries of history, and a dark story passed from one generation to another had all become intertwined. Standing in my imagination beneath the old chapel, I could almost picture the hill as it might have looked centuries ago. Whether the three kings ever existed or not, their legend had become inseparable from the place. And perhaps that is the real reason Three Kings Chapel became “haunted”—not because anyone has proved that ghosts live there, but because generations of people have kept the story alive.

* The legend of the three dead kings:** The most famous story surrounding the chapel says that three powerful kings once ruled the area and constantly fought because each wanted to become the sole ruler. According to the legend, one king invited the other two to a meal and secretly poisoned them. After realizing what he had done and fearing the consequences, he is said to have consumed poison himself. Local folklore claims that all three kings died on the hill, and their spirits were never able to leave the place. * The hill is believed to be haunted after dark:** Three Kings Chapel stands alone on Cuelim Hill, surrounded by greenery and away from the busy areas of Goa. Its isolated position has contributed heavily to its eerie reputation. Local stories claim that people have experienced an unexplained feeling of being watched or sensed a strange presence around the chapel, particularly in the evening. However, there is no verified evidence that these experiences are paranormal. * The ghosts are said to remain on the hill:** According to the legend, the spirits of the three kings supposedly continue to wander around the chapel grounds. Some versions of the story say that the kings were buried at the site, which is why their spirits are believed to remain attached to the hill. This detail is part of folklore rather than documented history, but it has become one of the most chilling parts of the chapel's reputation. * The real history is older than the ghost story:** The chapel was founded in 1599 by Jesuit priest Father Gonçalo Carvalho and was originally known as the Chapel of Our Lady of Remedies. The present-day building is associated with a later reconstruction, while the ghost legend developed separately. The contrast between a centuries-old place of worship and a frightening local legend is one reason the chapel has become so mysterious. * The name “Three Kings” has another meaning:** One of the strangest facts is that the chapel's name is not actually based on the three ghostly kings from the haunting legend. It is connected to the Biblical Magi, traditionally known as the Three Kings. Every year on January 6, Goa celebrates the Feast of the Three Kings, making the chapel a place of religious celebration as well as a location surrounded by ghost stories. * The beautiful location makes the legend even creepier:** From the hilltop, visitors can see greenery, palm trees, fields and the Arabian Sea. During daylight, the scenery can look peaceful and beautiful. Yet the same isolation that makes the view spectacular has also helped create the chapel's eerie reputation. The silence, empty surroundings and old structure can make ordinary sounds seem mysterious, which may have helped the ghost stories survive for generations. * There is no solid proof that the three kings ever existed:** Perhaps the creepiest fact is also the biggest mystery: historians have found no reliable evidence that three Portuguese kings actually ruled from this hill. Goa was governed by Portuguese colonial authorities rather than three kings based at this chapel. This means the famous story should be treated as a local legend, not established history. Still, the lack of a definite explanation has helped keep the mystery alive. * The chapel has become famous as a “haunted” place despite being a real place of worship:** Beneath all the ghost stories, Three Kings Chapel remains a functioning religious site with a genuine centuries-old history. That contrast is what makes it so fascinating: a peaceful chapel dedicated to Our Lady of Remedies has become famous across Goa for a dark story that may have no historical basis at all. The legend has become almost as famous as the chapel itself.
